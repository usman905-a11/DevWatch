## Problem and decision

M23's structurally valid, four-field causal review accepted an unsupported SQL
authorization consequence and an alternative copy fix that drops order fields.
M24 also retained an unqualified scheduling-impact inference. Schema completeness
alone cannot establish semantic truth. A keyword blacklist or a fabricated
default assessment would conceal this limitation rather than solve it.

Add a bounded counterexample pass to positive canonical verifications using the
same Synthesizer/Verifier identity. Its first judgments remain provisional and
cannot reach final assembly or checkpoint recovery. The next pass sees the same
candidate-bound source and claim, with guidance to falsify unsupported impact and
every proposed fix alternative; it is not shown the first pass's rationale.
Publication requires conservative reconciliation. Any rejection vetoes a positive
finding; uncertainty cannot silently become confirmation. If passes disagree,
unproven batch relations cannot merge conflicting claims. Supported narrower
claims may be recovered only through the existing bounded follow-up mechanism.

This is a correlated model cross-check, not independent human adjudication or a
proof of truth. No eighth identity, budget reset or extra retry is introduced.
Canonical execution must opt into the stricter policy, while direct low-level
subgraph callers retain their existing contract until explicitly configured.

## Checkpoint and batching decisions

The stricter policy is always enabled by canonical execution. A legacy in-flight
checkpoint cannot reuse an unchallenged positive output after the policy changes;
its decisions must enter the provisional stage again, without resetting elapsed
time, tokens or iterations. Deadline recovery may retain only judgments admitted
under the current policy. Completed historical reports remain historical records.

A pending challenge pins the original candidate IDs. If prompt bounds split that
batch, reconcile only the delivered subset and retain the rest as provisional.
Do not carry duplicate/disagreement relations across an unreviewed partition.
Selected decisions use the weaker verdict and lower confidence of the two passes.
Any disagreement drops relation claims rather than silently resolving them.
